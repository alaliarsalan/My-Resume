LAYER 2 Cold Send Path

1
▼
Enrolment
prospect → sequence
A prospect joins a sequence. Timezone is resolved from the location field against a 100+ country mapper, so the sequence can send in their business hours, not the sender's. Consent is recorded with a type and an expiry date, not a boolean.
location → IANA tzconsent type + expiryOUT: enrolled step 1 queued
2
▼
Send-time gate
seven checks before any email
Outside the prospect's window? Skip. Daily or monthly cap hit? Skip. Consent expired, prospect on the global blocklist, or already replied? Skip. The gate is evaluated per email at send time, not at campaign launch, because conditions change while a sequence runs.
tz windowdaily capconsent expiryblockliststop-on-replyOUT: eligible or deferred
3
▼
Personalisation and per-recipient uniqueness
render step
Merge fields resolved, optional AI icebreaker pulled from cached enrichment, and a spintax block expanded per recipient so no two bodies are byte-identical. Open tracking is off by default: a tracking pixel is a spam signal and Apple's mail privacy makes open data unreliable anyway.
{{FirstName}}spintax {a|b}icebreaker (optional)OUT: final message body
4
▼
Outbox commit — the exactly-once guarantee
before the network is touched
An outbox row with a unique idempotency key is committed before the send. If the process dies between SMTP and the audit write, the retry finds the key and does nothing. This single ordering decision removed an entire class of duplicate-email incidents.
INSERT outbox (unique key)status: sent | failedOUT: idempotent send intent
Why it matters: cold email has one unforgivable failure mode, and it is receiving the same message twice from an automated system that claims to be careful.
5
▼
Send through the tenant's own mailbox
orchestrator, not sender
Delivery goes through the customer's mailbox: Gmail API, Microsoft Graph, or any SMTP provider. Randomized human pacing between sends. If the send fails, it retries three times with backoff before surfacing a failure.
Gmail APIGraph APISMTPOUT: delivered or retry ×3
6
Outcome and automatic guardrails
the part that protects the domain
On success the next step is scheduled. On a rolling 24-hour window, an account that crosses the bounce or complaint threshold is paused automatically and its daily limit halved, before the domain reputation takes the damage. Thresholds match what the mailbox providers actually enforce, not the looser defaults most tools ship with.
bounce thresholdcomplaint thresholdauto-pausenotificationOUT: healthy sending reputation
Design stance: the deliverability guardrails are not features the customer can switch off, because the failure is invisible until the inbox placement is already gone.

Autonomous routing
per category, no human in the loop
Unsubscribe cancels every sequence and writes a consent record. Hostile pauses and blocks. Out-of-office pauses for a week. Referral tags and notifies. Interested flags the inbox. Auto-replies are recognised and suppressed instead of being treated as engagement.

CRM handoff
signed webhooks
Interested replies fire a signed webhook to Slack, Discord, HubSpot or Zapier. Payloads carry an HMAC-SHA256 signature, retries use backoff, every attempt is logged, and an endpoint that keeps failing is disabled rather than hammered.

KEY Decisions, Trade-offs, Scope

Exactly-once sends. Outbox row before SMTP. Cost: one extra write per email. Benefit: duplicates become structurally impossible.
Keyword first, model second. Autonomy that depends on a model call is autonomy that fails when the model does.
No broker, no Redis. In-process scheduler with a leader lock. Correct at this scale, and the first thing to replace at the next one.
Consent as an audit trail. Append-only, expiring, and enforced at send time. A boolean flag cannot survive an audit.
Orchestrator, not sender. Sending through tenant mailboxes keeps the VPS out of the delivery path and out of the reputation conversation.
Verification over claims. Every scheduled job has a health check, every deploy gets a live smoke test, and no change ships on an agent's word alone.
